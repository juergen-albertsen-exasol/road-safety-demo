# Context: the Mobility Data Space and Data for Road Safety

This demo shows, on a live map of Munich, how safety-related traffic information is created from vehicle and road sensor data. It follows the **Data for Road Safety (DFRS)** use case of the **Mobility Data Space (MDS)**. This page summarises the background from the MDS presentation "Mobility Data Space" (DRM Datenraum Mobilität GmbH, 29 October 2026) and explains how the demo maps to it.

## The Mobility Data Space

The MDS is an independent data space for mobility data. Its partners use its sovereign infrastructure to develop and run data-driven mobility services. It is operated by DRM Datenraum Mobilität GmbH in Munich, with funding from the German Federal Ministry of Transport by decision of the German Bundestag.

The MDS presents itself as one equation:

> **partners and community + mobility-related data + trusted infrastructure = innovative services, products and analyses**

**Partners and community.** More than 150 members from automotive, mobility and traffic services, logistics, insurance, the public sector, smart cities, tourism and public transport, consulting and IT, and research. The majority shareholder, acatech Stiftung, anchors trust and neutrality. Other shareholders include carmakers, Deutsche Bahn, DHL, HERE, Caruso, HUK and the states of Bavaria, Baden-Württemberg and North Rhine-Westphalia.

**Mobility-related data.** The data catalogue lists several hundred offers in categories such as vehicle and telematics data, road works and road conditions, traffic, parking, electromobility, traffic signs and speed, weather and environment, public transport, car and bike sharing, and infrastructure. Examples close to this demo: event datasets from carmakers for *Breakdown*, *Dangerous Slowdown* (triggered by signals such as the electronic stability control) and *Accident* (triggered by the emergency call), multi-brand in-vehicle data, *Hazard Warnings* and a *Road Hazard Score* per road segment.

**Trusted infrastructure.** Five governance principles:

| Principle | What it means in the MDS |
|---|---|
| Transparency | A catalogue describes every data offer (metadata) and is the basis for matching providers and users |
| Trust | A neutral intermediary ensures the origin and authenticity of the data; the exchange between connectors is tamper-proof |
| Data sovereignty | Providers decide whether, with whom and under which conditions they share |
| Interoperability | IDS reference architecture, Gaia-X Lighthouse project, Eclipse Dataspace Connector (EDC) technology |
| Decentralisation | Data isn't stored centrally; participants exchange it directly through their connectors |

Technically, every member runs a connector (EDC). The MDS authority provides the portal, the data catalogue, authentication and a logging house. Behind a member's connector sits its own backend for data storage, analytics and digital tools: the place where a database like Exasol fits.

## Data for Road Safety (DFRS)

**Goal:** improve road safety by **maximising the reach of safety-related traffic information**, generated by vehicles and infrastructure. Data from more than 8 million connected vehicles is already integrated. Participants include carmakers, navigation and traffic service providers, and road authorities from several European countries (see <https://www.dataforroadsafety.eu/partners>).

**Shared data: the eight categories of Safety-Related Traffic Information (SRTI),** as defined by Delegated Regulation (EU) No 886/2013:

1. Temporary slippery road
2. Animal, people, obstacles, debris on the road
3. Unprotected accident area
4. Short-term road works
5. Reduced visibility
6. Wrong-way driver
7. Unmanaged blockage of a road
8. Exceptional weather conditions

**Implementation in the MDS.** Each participant (carmakers, service providers, road authorities) connects through an MDS connector with a Kafka extension, so events flow as streams between the parties. The MDS authority provides the portal, the data catalogue, an observer and the identity service (DAPS). MDS services for DFRS: a standardised interface, the Kafka extension, a logging mechanism, connector-as-a-service, the latest EDC version, and data sovereignty.

## How the demo maps to this

| DFRS / MDS | In the demo |
|---|---|
| Carmakers and service providers sharing vehicle events | Six fictional carmakers (OEM A–F) and a navigation service, each with its own fleet of connected *probe vehicles*. Generic names on purpose: no real brand data is behind them. Not every provider shares every signal, so a hazard often needs more than one provider to be confirmed |
| Road authorities sharing infrastructure data | A road operator with 158 virtual sensors at real places: road weather stations on bridges, wrong-way cameras at motorway entries, tunnel cameras, loop detectors, and warning trailers at short-term road works |
| The event streams (Kafka) | The simulator writes the vehicle signals and sensor readings into Exasol in micro-batches every 2 seconds (schema `ROAD_SAFETY`) |
| The eight SRTI categories | Used one to one: every incident on the map is one of them |
| A service provider that turns raw events into safety information | SQL in Exasol: rules per category confirm a hazard from several vehicles, providers and sensors within a radius and a time window, give it a confidence, and keep the evidence chain |
| Maximising the reach | For every incident the SQL counts the vehicles within 2 km heading towards it, the ones that would receive the warning |
| Data catalogue, open mobility data | The background is real open data: OpenStreetMap road network, City of Munich roadworks, Autobahn GmbH motorway events, DWD weather, BASt traffic counts, the accident atlas |

**What the demo doesn't show:** connectors, the Kafka extension, identity, usage policies or the logging house. The demo starts where the data has arrived in a participant's backend, and shows what that participant can do with it in a database.

**What is real and what is simulated.** The road network, roadworks, motorway events, weather, traffic volumes and accident history are real open data. The vehicle and sensor signals are synthetic, because no open source of raw vehicle signals exists and DFRS data isn't published under an open license. The presenter starts every hazard on purpose, and the page always shows that it is simulated. The detection SQL never reads which hazards were started: it finds them only from the signals.
